Dear Guest Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, ``ProcRosetta: Behavior-Supervised Alignment and Retrieval of Heterogeneous Process Mining Artifacts'', for the Applied Sciences Special Issue ``Process Mining: Theory and Applications''.

The manuscript studies how to compare and retrieve behaviorally related event logs, process trees, and Petri nets despite their different structures. ProcRosetta organizes heterogeneous examples into behavior families and trains modality-specific encoders in a shared space using relation-aware supervision. On the fixed 48-family held-out subset, directional cross-artifact Recall@1 ranges from 0.656 to 0.875 under the reported multi-observation protocol. These values are explicitly scoped to that protocol and do not imply full-test or deduplicated-repository performance.

The work fits the Special Issue through its treatment of event logs, process model retrieval, AI-assisted process discovery support, validation through conformance checking, and formal analysis of computational costs. Its intended application is a workflow that retrieves, proposes, and validates: an analyst queries a heterogeneous repository, optionally requests a process tree candidate, and subjects that candidate to established conformance checking, soundness checks, and behavioral checks before acceptance.

The grammar-constrained decoder guarantees well-formed process tree expressions and their representational conversion to Petri nets under the stated assumptions. These properties do not guarantee behavioral preservation. The manuscript retains the negative generation and external event log results and shows that direct event log to process tree generation is currently weaker than the reported Inductive Miner baseline. ProcRosetta is presented as an alignment, retrieval, and candidate-proposal framework, not as a replacement for process discovery or conformance checking.

The source code, generated data, trained model, and inspection interface are publicly available.
