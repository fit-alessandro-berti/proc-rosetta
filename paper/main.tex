\begin{abstract}
Process mining revolves around heterogeneous artifacts: event logs record observed behavior, while process trees and Petri nets specify intended or discovered behavior. Every established technique that connects two of these artifact types---discovery, conformance checking, model transformation---is a dedicated algorithm for one direction of one artifact pair. This paper presents \emph{ProcRosetta}, a multimodal neural framework that embeds event logs, process trees, and Petri nets into a single shared latent space of process behavior and decodes any point of that space into a syntactically valid process tree. Three artifact-specific encoders (a recurrent tree encoder, a hierarchical attention-based log encoder, and a message-passing Petri-net encoder) are trained jointly with a shared grammar-masked decoder on synthetic paired triples, using reconstruction, cross-modal translation, latent alignment, contrastive, and variational objectives; grammar masking and deterministic tree-to-net conversion make every generated model well-formed by construction. On a held-out test set of 512 process triples, ProcRosetta decodes valid, Petri-convertible process trees from all three modalities in 100\% of the cases, translates unseen event logs into process models that reach an alignment-based F1 of 0.787 (compared to 0.857 for the Inductive Miner), retrieves cross-modal counterparts far above chance level, and produces 64-dimensional embeddings whose geometry correlates with behavioral distance on par with much higher-dimensional deterministic log representations. The implementation, data generator, and evaluation suite are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Process mining connects observed event data with formal process models~\cite{vanDerAalst2016} and constantly moves between different \emph{representations} of the same behavior: an event log is a finite sample of executed traces, a process tree is a block-structured hierarchical specification, and a Petri net is a graph-based execution model with explicit states and concurrency. The bridges between these views are dedicated, hand-designed algorithms, each for one direction of one artifact pair: discovery maps logs to models~\cite{Aalst2004,Leemans2013,Augusto2019Split}, conformance checking relates logs and models through alignments~\cite{Adriansyah2012}, and model-to-model transformations connect notations such as workflow nets and process trees~\cite{VanZelst2020}. What is missing is a common \emph{computational representation} in which a log, a tree, and a net describing the same behavior are simply \emph{the same point}. Such a representation would enable similarity search and clustering across artifact types, provide fixed-length behavioral feature vectors for downstream machine learning, and turn translation between artifact types into a decode operation. In machine learning, embedding heterogeneous modalities such as images and text into one shared vector space has proven remarkably effective~\cite{Radford2021,Baltrusaitis2019}; process mining has adopted representation learning for traces and logs~\cite{DeKoninck2018,Barbon2023} and for Petri nets~\cite{Colonna2024}, but these efforts remain \emph{single-modality}, and none of them can generate a process model back from the learned space.

This paper presents \textbf{ProcRosetta}, a multimodal neural framework that treats event logs, process trees, and Petri nets as three views of one process behavior. ProcRosetta trains three artifact-specific encoders into a shared latent space, aligns the views of the same process through explicit alignment and contrastive objectives, and attaches a single shared decoder that maps any latent vector back to a process tree. The decoder is \emph{grammar-masked}: at every generation step, only tokens that keep the partial output a syntactically valid process tree are allowed, following the philosophy of grammar-constrained decoding~\cite{Kusner2017,Geng2023}; decoded trees are converted to Petri nets deterministically using PM4Py~\cite{Berti2023PM4Py}, so every generated model is well-formed and sound by construction. The name is an analogy to the Rosetta stone: the training data is the same content in three ``languages'', and the model learns the correspondence.

Figure~\ref{fig:outline} summarizes the framework. Three research questions organize the investigation:

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] Can event logs, process trees, and Petri nets be embedded into a shared latent space in which the three views of the same process behavior receive nearby representations?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] Can a learned model translate event logs or Petri nets into \emph{valid} process trees---well-formed, Petri-convertible models whose behavior is close to the source?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] How do the learned representations and the learned log-to-model translation compare with classical baselines: deterministic log and model features, Petri-net embeddings, and the Inductive Miner?
\end{itemize}

The contributions are: (i)~a multimodal architecture with three artifact-specific encoders projecting into one latent space of process behavior; (ii)~a shared, grammar-masked process-tree decoder that guarantees syntactic validity of every generated model and, through deterministic conversion, yields sound Petri nets; (iii)~a training procedure over synthetic paired triples combining reconstruction, cross-modal translation, latent alignment, contrastive, and variational objectives; and (iv)~a comprehensive assessment with a publicly available implementation, covering decode quality, discovery quality against the Inductive Miner, behavior-aligned embedding quality, and cross-modal retrieval.

Section~\ref{sec:relatedWork} reviews related work; Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} introduces the process-mining objects; Section~\ref{sec:approach} presents the framework; Section~\ref{sec:assessment} reports the assessment; Sections~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss and conclude.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedWork}

\paragraph{Process discovery and block-structured models.}
Automated process discovery derives a process model from an event log~\cite{Aalst2004}; a large benchmark over real-life logs showed that no single algorithm dominates across fitness, precision, generalization, and simplicity~\cite{Augusto2019Review}. The Inductive Miner~\cite{Leemans2013} discovers \emph{process trees} by recursive log decomposition, yielding block-structured models that are sound by construction; process trees are a standard pivot representation with formal conversions to Petri nets and BPMN~\cite{Leemans2026}, and workflow nets can be translated back to process trees when possible~\cite{VanZelst2020}. Beyond strict block structure, Split Miner~\cite{Augusto2019Split} balances accuracy and simplicity, and the POWL family~\cite{Kourani2023POWL,Kourani2026} discovers partially ordered workflow models that are more expressive than process trees while preserving soundness. ProcRosetta shares the Inductive Miner's process-tree bias---the decoder emits process trees because their grammar guarantees well-formedness and their Petri-net conversion is deterministic---but instead of a deterministic log-to-model algorithm, it learns a shared representation over logs, trees, and nets from which models can be decoded.

\paragraph{Model similarity, conformance, and behavioral distances.}
Similarity of process models has been measured over labels, structure, and behavior~\cite{Dijkman2011}, with the recurring insight that behavioral closeness cannot be reduced to structural resemblance; alignment-based conformance checking~\cite{Adriansyah2012} provides exact, interpretable comparisons between logs and models. ProcRosetta learns a \emph{continuous proxy} for such comparisons---attractive for scalable retrieval and clustering, but without formal guarantees---so the assessment measures explicitly how well the learned geometry agrees with a behavioral distance (Section~\ref{sec:embeddingresults}).

\paragraph{Representation learning for process-mining artifacts.}
Representation learning entered process mining chiefly through single-modality embeddings: act2vec, trace2vec, log2vec, and model2vec~\cite{DeKoninck2018} and a rich design space of trace encodings~\cite{Barbon2023}. Deep sequence models for predictive monitoring~\cite{RamaManeiro2023} learn powerful trace representations but target prediction over running cases rather than model generation or cross-artifact alignment. On the model side, Petri nets are graphs, making random-walk embeddings such as node2vec~\cite{Grover2016} and graph neural networks~\cite{Kipf2017} relevant. Most directly related is PetriNet2Vec by Colonna et al.~\cite{Colonna2024}, which learns unsupervised vector representations of Petri nets and serves as a baseline in our assessment. All of these approaches embed \emph{one} artifact family; ProcRosetta's distinctive step is to align three artifact families in one space and to couple the space with a generative decoder.

\paragraph{Neural and LLM-based process-model generation.}
Sommers et al.~\cite{Sommers2021} train graph neural networks on synthetic log--model pairs to discover sound Petri nets; this is the closest neural predecessor, though it addresses one direction (log$\to$net) rather than a tri-modal space. More recently, large language models have been used to generate process models from natural-language descriptions~\cite{Kourani2024LLM}, also specialized via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards~\cite{Berti2025RL}. These approaches are language-native, whereas ProcRosetta is artifact-native---its inputs are logs, trees, and nets themselves---so the directions are complementary.

\paragraph{Multimodal learning and grammar-constrained decoding.}
Outside process mining, multimodal learning studies shared representations across modalities~\cite{Baltrusaitis2019}; CLIP~\cite{Radford2021} demonstrated contrastive alignment of paired images and captions at scale. ProcRosetta transfers this recipe to process mining, with the twist that its ``modalities'' are formal artifacts whose equivalence is behavioral rather than perceptual. For generation, grammar-constrained decoding guarantees syntactic validity of structured outputs, as in grammar variational autoencoders~\cite{Kusner2017} and grammar-constrained LLM decoding~\cite{Geng2023}; ProcRosetta applies this principle to process trees, combining ideas that have so far been separate---process-tree soundness, graph encoders for Petri nets, log encoders, contrastive multimodal alignment, and grammar-constrained decoding---in a single trainable framework.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

We fix a finite alphabet of activity labels $\Sigma$. Throughout the paper, activity labels are \emph{canonicalized} to $\mathrm{A0}, \mathrm{A1}, \ldots$ in order of first occurrence, making all representations invariant to arbitrary activity naming while preserving repeated-activity identity.

\paragraph{Event logs.}
A \emph{trace} is a finite word $\sigma = \langle a_1, \ldots, a_m \rangle$ with $a_j \in \Sigma$; an \emph{event log} is a finite multiset of traces, $L = [\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n] \in \mathcal{B}(\Sigma^*)$, preserving trace frequencies. Logs carry only control-flow information here, the classical control-flow abstraction of richer formats such as XES~\cite{IEEE1849-2023}.

\paragraph{Process trees.}
A \emph{process tree}~\cite{Leemans2013,Leemans2026} is a block-structured process model generated by the grammar
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
T \;::=\;& a \;\mid\; \tau \;\mid\; \operatorname{SEQ}(T_1,\ldots,T_k) \;\mid\; \operatorname{XOR}(T_1,\ldots,T_k)\\
&\mid\; \operatorname{AND}(T_1,\ldots,T_k) \;\mid\; \operatorname{LOOP}(T_{\mathit{body}},T_{\mathit{redo}},T_{\mathit{exit}})
\end{split}
\label{eq:grammar}
\end{equation}
with $k \ge 2$, where $a \in \Sigma$ is a visible activity, $\tau$ is a silent step, $\operatorname{SEQ}$ executes its children in order, $\operatorname{XOR}$ selects exactly one child, $\operatorname{AND}$ interleaves its children, and $\operatorname{LOOP}$ repeats a body with a redo and an exit part. Every process tree can be converted into a sound workflow net~\cite{Leemans2013}. Since $\operatorname{XOR}$ and $\operatorname{AND}$ are commutative, their children are sorted by a canonical structural key, giving behaviorally identical permutations one canonical representation.

\paragraph{Petri nets.}
An \emph{accepting labeled Petri net}~\cite{vanDerAalst2016} is a tuple $\mathit{PN} = (P, T, F, \ell, M_0, M_f)$ with places $P$, transitions $T$ ($P \cap T = \emptyset$), flow relation $F \subseteq (P \times T) \cup (T \times P)$, labeling $\ell : T \to \Sigma \cup \{\tau\}$, and initial and final markings $M_0, M_f$. For neural encoding, a Petri net is represented as a \emph{typed graph}: each node carries a type (place, visible transition, or invisible transition) and its token counts in $M_0$ and $M_f$; each arc carries a direction type. Nodes are sorted by name before tensorization, making the representation deterministic.

\section{The ProcRosetta Framework}
\label{sec:approach}

This section presents the paired training data, the encoders, the grammar-masked decoder, and the training objective, briefly explaining the neural building blocks where they appear.

\subsection{Synthetic Paired Triples}
\label{sec:datagen}

Real-world collections rarely record which log, tree, and net represent the \emph{same} process, so ProcRosetta is trained on synthetic \emph{paired triples} in which the correspondence holds by construction (Figure~\ref{fig:triple}): each sample $x_i = (T_i, L_i, G_i)$ is derived from one randomly generated process tree $T_i$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $T_i$ is sampled by a recursive generator that at each node emits an activity leaf (probability $0.35$, always at maximum depth $3$) or an operator from $\{\operatorname{SEQ}, \operatorname{XOR}, \operatorname{AND}\}$ with arity $2$ or $3$; labels come from at most $30$ activities (reuse probability $0.15$), and the tree is canonicalized (Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries});
  \item $L_i$ is obtained by playing out $T_i$ with PM4Py's process-tree simulation ($16$ traces per sample);
  \item $G_i$ is the typed-graph representation of the Petri net obtained by PM4Py's deterministic conversion of $T_i$.
\end{itemize}
The generator uses a fixed random seed and produces three disjoint splits: $4{,}000$ training, $512$ validation, and $512$ test triples (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). The grammar and the architecture support the $\operatorname{LOOP}$ operator as well (Equation~\ref{eq:grammar}); the experiments reported here use the operator set $\{\operatorname{SEQ}, \operatorname{XOR}, \operatorname{AND}\}$, which covers sequence, exclusive choice, and concurrency.

For neural processing, each artifact is \emph{tokenized}. A process tree becomes a prefix sequence over a $40$-token vocabulary (special tokens, operators, arities, and activities $\mathrm{A0}, \ldots, \mathrm{A29}$), where each operator is followed by an explicit arity token and then its children (Figure~\ref{fig:triple}b). Traces are sequences over the $31$-token activity vocabulary. The Petri graph is tensorized into node-type indices, marking features, and two directed adjacency matrices (one per arc direction).

\subsection{Three Encoders, One Latent Space}
\label{sec:encoders}

Each encoder maps its artifact to a diagonal Gaussian $q_m(z \mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_m, \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_m^2))$ over a shared $D_z$-dimensional latent space ($D_z = 64$), for modality $m \in \{\mathit{tree}, \mathit{trace}, \mathit{petri}\}$, as in variational autoencoders~\cite{KingmaWelling2014}: $\mu_m$ is the embedding of the artifact and $\sigma_m$ the encoder's uncertainty. During training, latent vectors are sampled as $z_m = \mu_m + \epsilon \odot \sigma_m$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, which regularizes the space; at test time the deterministic mean $z_m = \mu_m$ is used.

\emph{Tree encoder.} The token sequence of the process tree is embedded and processed by a gated recurrent unit (GRU)---a standard recurrent network that reads a sequence left to right while maintaining a hidden state---with hidden size $H = 128$; the hidden state at the last non-padding token is projected linearly to $(\mu_{\mathit{tree}}, \sigma_{\mathit{tree}})$.

\emph{Log encoder.} The event-log encoder is \emph{hierarchical}, mirroring the log/trace/event structure: a GRU first encodes each trace into a trace vector; an attention mechanism then computes a data-dependent weight for every trace---emphasizing informative trace variants rather than averaging uniformly---and pools the trace vectors into a single log vector, projected to $(\mu_{\mathit{trace}}, \sigma_{\mathit{trace}})$.

\emph{Petri-net encoder.} The Petri-net encoder is a message-passing graph network~\cite{Kipf2017}. Each node starts from an embedding of its type (place / visible transition / invisible transition) plus a linear encoding of its marking feature; in each of three message-passing rounds, every node updates its vector from its own state and its incoming and outgoing neighbors, followed by a nonlinearity and layer normalization. Mean-pooling over nodes yields the graph vector, projected to $(\mu_{\mathit{petri}}, \sigma_{\mathit{petri}})$. Transition labels are not used as node features, so this encoder captures the \emph{structure} of the net.

When several views are available, ProcRosetta also defines a \emph{fused} embedding, the mean $\mu_{\mathit{fused}} = \tfrac{1}{3}(\mu_{\mathit{tree}} + \mu_{\mathit{trace}} + \mu_{\mathit{petri}})$.

\subsection{Grammar-Masked Process-Tree Decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}

A single decoder, shared by all three modalities, is an autoregressive GRU: the latent vector $z$ initializes its hidden state, and tokens are generated one at a time, each step predicting a distribution over the vocabulary given the tokens so far.

An unconstrained decoder could produce malformed sequences (e.g., an operator without children). ProcRosetta therefore applies a \emph{grammar mask} derived from a prefix grammar of process trees. The decoding state tracks the number of open node slots and whether an arity token is pending: after $\texttt{<bos>}$, exactly one node is open; an activity or $\tau$ closes one slot; an operator token must be followed by an arity token $\mathrm{ARITY}\_k$, which opens $k$ child slots; when no slots remain open, only $\texttt{<eos>}$ is valid. At every step, the logits (pre-softmax scores) of all grammar-invalid tokens are set to $-10^9$, so invalid continuations receive zero probability during training and greedy decoding. Consequently, \emph{every} terminated output parses into a process tree of grammar~(\ref{eq:grammar}), and PM4Py's deterministic conversion turns it into a sound Petri net---transferring the validity-by-construction philosophy of grammar-based molecular and program generation~\cite{Kusner2017,Geng2023} to process models.

\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:training}

Training uses \emph{teacher forcing}: the decoder receives the ground-truth token sequence shifted by one position and learns to predict every next token. Since all three artifacts of a triple share the same target tree, each mini-batch trains three decoding pathways through the same decoder---reconstruction ($z_{\mathit{tree}} \to T$) and two cross-modal translations ($z_{\mathit{trace}} \to T$, $z_{\mathit{petri}} \to T$)---via grammar-masked sequence cross-entropies ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{tree}\to\mathit{tree}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{trace}\to\mathit{tree}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{petri}\to\mathit{tree}}$) with label smoothing $0.05$ distributed only over grammar-valid tokens.

Two objectives shape the latent space. The \emph{alignment loss} penalizes disagreement between the latent means of the same sample,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{align}} = \tfrac{1}{3}\big( \lVert\mu_{\mathit{tree}}-\mu_{\mathit{trace}}\rVert_2^2 + \lVert\mu_{\mathit{tree}}-\mu_{\mathit{petri}}\rVert_2^2 + \lVert\mu_{\mathit{trace}}-\mu_{\mathit{petri}}\rVert_2^2 \big),
\end{equation}
pulling the three views of one process toward the same point. The \emph{contrastive loss}, in the style of CLIP~\cite{Radford2021}, additionally pushes \emph{different} processes apart: for every modality pair within a mini-batch, a symmetric cross-entropy over cosine similarities (temperature $0.2$) rewards each artifact for being most similar to its own counterpart rather than to the other samples in the batch. Finally, a weak KL regularizer $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{KL}}$ toward a standard normal prior~\cite{KingmaWelling2014} keeps the latent space bounded and smooth. The total loss is the weighted sum
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{tree}\to\mathit{tree}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathit{trace}\to\mathit{tree}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathit{petri}\to\mathit{tree}}}_{\text{reconstruction and translation}} + \; 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{align}} + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{contrastive}} + 0.001\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{KL}}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

\section{Assessment}
\label{sec:assessment}

The assessment follows the three research questions, covering training behavior, decode quality (RQ2), discovery quality against the Inductive Miner (RQ3), embedding quality against baselines (RQ1, RQ3), and cross-modal retrieval (RQ1). All results are computed on the held-out test split of $512$ triples---used neither for optimization nor for model selection---with the best-validation checkpoint and deterministic latent means.

\subsection{Training Behavior}
\label{sec:trainingbehavior}



Figure~\ref{fig:training} shows the training run. Training converged smoothly: the validation loss decreased from $3.75$ to $2.64$, the schedule halved the learning rate three times (epochs 27, 31, and 38), and early stopping ended the run after epoch 40; the best-validation checkpoint (epoch 35) is used throughout the assessment. The training--validation gap stays small (about $0.23$ at the end), indicating that the regularization suite of Table~\ref{tab:hyper} prevents substantial overfitting. All loss components improve together (Figure~\ref{fig:training}b): reconstruction is the easiest task; the two translation losses follow at a higher level, as expected, since a log of 16 sampled traces and a label-free net structure carry less information about the target tree than the tree itself; and the contrastive loss drops steeply in the first epochs, showing that separating different processes in latent space is learned early. The test loss of $2.618$ (tree reconstruction $0.691$, trace$\to$tree $0.917$, Petri$\to$tree $0.837$, contrastive $1.120$) closely matches the validation loss. Training takes about 30 minutes on a single CPU; no GPU is required at this scale.

The semantic half is more nuanced. The decoded tree \emph{exactly} equals the canonical target in 48.4\% of the cases when decoding from the tree's own embedding, 40.0\% from the log, and 35.9\% from the Petri-net structure. Exact match is demanding (a single differing leaf counts as failure); the error-tolerant metrics are more informative: the normalized token edit distance is low (0.18--0.29), and the \emph{behavioral} distance between the original log and traces simulated from the decoded model ranges from 0.675 to 0.839, whereas the same distance between two \emph{unrelated} test logs averages 1.566 (Section~\ref{sec:embeddingresults})---decoded models are behaviorally about twice as close to their source as a random model of the same distribution. The Inductive Miner achieves perfect fitness (1.000) and an F1 of 0.857. This is expected: the test logs are complete, noise-free playouts of block-structured trees---precisely the setting in which the Inductive Miner enjoys rediscovery guarantees~\cite{Leemans2013}---so the comparison is intentionally played on its home turf. ProcRosetta reaches a fitness of 0.841, a precision of 0.763, and an F1 of 0.787---about 92\% of the Inductive Miner's F1. Notably, a purely neural translation---the entire log is compressed into a single 64-dimensional vector, with no search, replay, or counting---lands within this range while producing a valid model for every log. The gap is concentrated in fitness: a slightly wrong decoded tree makes some observed traces non-replayable, whereas the Inductive Miner guarantees their inclusion. We interpret this not as a claim that neural translation should replace classical discovery, but as evidence that the shared latent space preserves enough behavioral information to support discovery-grade model generation.

\subsection{Embedding Quality (RQ1, RQ3)}
\label{sec:embeddingresults}

If the latent space is a space of process \emph{behavior}, then distances in it should correlate with behavioral distances. The assessment quantifies the behavioral distance between two logs as the mean of three $L_1$ distances between empirical distributions extracted from the logs---trace variants, directly-follows relations (with boundary symbols)~\cite{vanDerAalst2019DFG}, and trace lengths---each in $[0,2]$. Across all $130{,}816$ pairs of test logs, this distance averages $1.566$ (min $0.0$, max $2.0$). Each embedding method is scored by: (i)~the Spearman rank correlation $\rho$ between embedding cosine distance and behavioral distance over all pairs; (ii)~the mean behavioral distance from each sample to its \emph{nearest neighbor} in the embedding (NN behavior; lower is better); and (iii)~the improvement of the latter over the random-pair average (higher is better).

\subsection{Cross-Modal Retrieval (RQ1)}
\label{sec:retrievalresults}



The most direct test of cross-modal alignment is retrieval: given, e.g., a Petri net, does its embedding find the matching event log among all 512 test logs? Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} reports top-1 accuracy, mean reciprocal rank (MRR), and the mean rank of the true counterpart, for all six directions. All directions perform far above chance: top-1 accuracies of 14.1--44.3\% against a 0.2\% chance level (factors of 70--220), and mean ranks of 32--49 against an expected 256 for random ranking. The pattern is informative: the tree--net pairing is easiest (both artifacts are complete structural descriptions), the tree--log pairing follows, and the log--net pairing is hardest---it connects the two \emph{indirect} views, a finite behavioral sample and a label-free graph structure, neither of which is the generative object. Distinguishing the one correct process from 511 same-distribution alternatives requires \emph{instance-level} matching, much finer than the neighborhood-level quality of Section~\ref{sec:embeddingresults}; the results establish that the shared space aligns modalities at this granularity well above chance, while leaving clear headroom.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Implementation}
\label{sec:repository}

ProcRosetta is implemented in Python on top of PyTorch and PM4Py~\cite{Berti2023PM4Py}; the complete source code (synthetic data generator, training and evaluation pipelines, and all metric implementations) is publicly available at \url{https://github.com/fit-alessandro-berti/proc-rosetta}. Three commands reproduce the study end-to-end: \texttt{sample.py} regenerates the seeded splits of Table~\ref{tab:dataset}, \texttt{train.py} trains the model of Table~\ref{tab:hyper} and writes the per-epoch metrics of Figure~\ref{fig:training}, and \texttt{test.py} produces every number in Tables~\ref{tab:decode}--\ref{tab:retrieval}. The repository also provides utilities that apply a trained checkpoint to external artifacts (\texttt{.xes} event logs, \texttt{.pnml} Petri nets, \texttt{.ptml} process trees), computing embeddings and decoding logs or nets into process trees with automatic canonicalization and label restoration.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
Four threats qualify these findings. \emph{(i) Synthetic data.} Training and evaluation use synthetic triples from random block-structured trees; real logs exhibit noise, incompleteness, deviating behavior, and long-tail variants absent from this distribution, so the conclusions concern in-distribution artifacts (out-of-distribution quality is not claimed here). \emph{(ii) Metric circularity.} The behavioral distance used for embedding evaluation shares two of its three components with the directly-follows and trace-variant baselines, inflating their scores; Table~\ref{tab:embedding} marks them, and the less affected nearest-neighbor quality is reported alongside correlation. \emph{(iii) Duplicate behavior across splits.} The generator relies on independent random generation rather than explicit behavioral deduplication, so a fraction of test behaviors may resemble training behaviors; the exact-match rates of Table~\ref{tab:decode} should be read with this in mind. \emph{(iv) Single configuration.} All results come from one seeded data configuration and one trained model; variance across seeds and hyperparameter sensitivity are not quantified.

\paragraph{Scope of the model class.}
The operator set used in the experiments covers sequence, exclusive choice, and concurrency; the grammar, tokenizer, and decoder already support the loop operator, so extending the trained model to loops is a data-configuration change rather than an architectural one. More fundamentally, the process-tree target restricts outputs to block-structured behavior---the restriction the Inductive Miner accepts in exchange for soundness---and is less expressive than partially ordered formalisms such as POWL~\cite{Kourani2023POWL,Kourani2026}. The architecture is modular here: any target language with a prefix grammar, a canonical form, and a deterministic conversion to Petri nets could replace the process-tree decoder.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced ProcRosetta, a multimodal neural framework that embeds the three central artifact types of process mining---event logs, process trees, and Petri nets---into a single latent space of process behavior and decodes any point of that space into a grammar-valid process tree that converts deterministically into a sound Petri net. The assessment answered all three research questions affirmatively and quantified the remaining headroom: the three encoders converge to one shared geometry that aligns paired artifacts at instance level far above chance (RQ1); grammar-masked decoding produced a valid, Petri-convertible process tree in 100\% of 2,048 test decodes, with decoded behavior roughly twice as close to the source as unrelated models (RQ2); and the learned 64-dimensional embeddings rival much higher-dimensional deterministic features while log-to-model translation reaches 92\% of the Inductive Miner's F1 in the miner's own best-case setting (RQ3).

Future work follows directly from the assessment. On the data side: larger and more diverse tree distributions including loops, noisy and incomplete simulated logs, quantified near-duplicate behavior across splits, and validation on real-life logs and model collections. On the model side: label-aware Petri-net encoding, beam or sampling-based decoding, multi-seed robustness studies, and scaling the encoders. On the representation side: richer sound target languages such as POWL~\cite{Kourani2026} as decoder output, an exact-conformance-based evaluation distance, and attaching further encoders---including natural-language process descriptions~\cite{Kourani2024LLM}---to the same latent space, moving closer to a true Rosetta stone for process representations.
