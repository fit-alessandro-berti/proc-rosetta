\begin{abstract}
Process mining relies on heterogeneous artifacts: event logs record observed behavior, while process trees and Petri nets describe intended or discovered behavior. Methods that connect these artifacts are typically specialized to a particular representation pair and task, which limits reuse across process discovery, comparison, retrieval, and translation. This paper presents \emph{ProcRosetta}, a multimodal neural framework that learns a shared latent space of process behavior for event logs, process trees, and Petri nets. Modality-specific encoders map each artifact into this space, while a shared grammar-masked decoder produces a grammar-valid process tree whenever decoding terminates, after which the process tree can be converted deterministically into a sound Petri net. The framework is trained on synthetic behaviorally paired views using complementary reconstruction, cross-modal translation, latent-alignment, contrastive, and variational objectives. We contribute the multimodal architecture, a controlled data-generation and training pipeline, and an assessment spanning decode validity, process discovery quality, behavior-aligned geometry, and cross-modal retrieval. Held-out results show that ProcRosetta reliably produces well-formed models, retains meaningful behavioral information during translation, and learns cross-modal neighborhoods that reflect process relationships. Specialized deterministic methods remain stronger on some tasks, positioning ProcRosetta as a reusable representation and translation layer rather than a replacement for established process mining algorithms. The implementation, data, and evaluation suite are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Process mining connects observed event data with formal models~\cite{vanDerAalst2016} and moves between different \emph{representations} of the same behavior: event logs sample executed traces, process trees specify block-structured behavior, and Petri nets make states and concurrency explicit. Existing bridges are direction-specific algorithms: process discovery maps event logs to models~\cite{Aalst2004,Leemans2013}, conformance checking relates event logs and models, and transformations connect model notations. What is missing is a common \emph{computational representation} in which equivalent artifacts are nearby regardless of type. Such a space would support cross-artifact search, clustering, machine learning, and translation by decoding. Shared embeddings are effective in multimodal learning~\cite{Radford2021}; process mining has learned representations for traces and event logs~\cite{DeKoninck2018,Barbon2023} and Petri nets~\cite{Colonna2024}, but these remain single-modality and do not generate models from the learned space.

ProcRosetta treats event logs, process trees, and Petri nets as three views of one behavior. Artifact-specific encoders map them into a shared latent space, alignment and contrastive objectives align matching views, and one decoder maps any latent vector to a process tree. The decoder is \emph{grammar-masked}: only tokens that keep the partial output syntactically valid are allowed~\cite{Geng2023}. Every completed process tree converts deterministically to a Petri net with PM4Py~\cite{Berti2023PM4Py}, making successfully terminated outputs well-formed and sound by construction. The name evokes the Rosetta stone: training presents the same content in three ``languages'' and learns their correspondence.

Figure~\ref{fig:outline} summarizes the framework. Three research questions organize the investigation:

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] Can event logs, process trees, and Petri nets be embedded into a shared latent space in which the three views of the same process behavior receive nearby representations?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] Can a learned model translate event logs or Petri nets into \emph{valid} process trees, namely well-formed models that are convertible to Petri nets and whose behavior is close to the source?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] How do the learned representations and the learned event-log-to-model translation compare with classical baselines: deterministic event-log and model features, Petri net embeddings, and the Inductive Miner?
\end{itemize}

The decoder always targets the block-structured process tree class. Petri net translation therefore denotes a learned behavioral projection into that class, not reconstruction of arbitrary Petri net topology; a non-block-structured input may decode to a structurally different sound Petri net.

The contributions are: (i)~a multimodal architecture with three artifact-specific encoders projecting into one latent space of process behavior; (ii)~a shared, grammar-masked process tree decoder that guarantees syntactic validity of completed outputs and, through deterministic conversion, yields sound Petri nets; (iii)~a training procedure over synthetic paired triples combining reconstruction, cross-modal translation, latent alignment, contrastive, and variational objectives; and (iv)~a comprehensive assessment with a publicly available implementation, covering decode quality, process discovery quality against the Inductive Miner, behavior-aligned embedding quality, and cross-modal retrieval.

Section~\ref{sec:relatedWork} reviews related work; Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} introduces the process mining objects; Section~\ref{sec:approach} presents the framework; Section~\ref{sec:tool} presents the interactive tool support; Section~\ref{sec:assessment} reports the assessment; Sections~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss and conclude.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedWork}

\paragraph{Process discovery and model generation.}
Classical process discovery maps event logs to models~\cite{Aalst2004}. The Inductive Miner recursively decomposes event logs into sound, block-structured process trees~\cite{Leemans2013}; more expressive sound targets such as POWL relax strict block structure~\cite{Kourani2026}. Neural process discovery has learned a direct event-log-to-Petri net mapping from synthetic pairs~\cite{Sommers2023}. ProcRosetta retains the process tree bias for validity and deterministic Petri conversion, but learns a tri-modal space rather than one event-log-to-model direction.

\paragraph{Representation learning.}
Prior process embeddings cover traces and event logs~\cite{DeKoninck2018,Barbon2023} or Petri nets~\cite{Colonna2024}; PetriNet2Vec is therefore a baseline below. They encode one artifact family and do not decode models from a shared space. ProcRosetta instead aligns three artifact types and evaluates both latent geometry and generated-model behavior.

Model comparison distinguishes label, structural, and behavioral similarity~\cite{Dijkman2011}, while alignment-based conformance provides exact event-log-to-model diagnostics~\cite{Adriansyah2013}. ProcRosetta's latent distance is a scalable but approximate proxy, so the assessment separately measures behavioral-distance agreement and discovered-model conformance.

\paragraph{Shared representations and constrained decoding.}
CLIP established contrastive alignment of paired modalities~\cite{Radford2021}, while grammar-constrained decoding enforces structured outputs~\cite{Geng2023}. ProcRosetta combines these ideas for formal artifacts: equivalence is behavioral, and the shared decoder emits grammar-valid process trees. Tri-modal alignment, behavioral evaluation, and validity-preserving generation distinguish it from both single-modality embeddings and direct neural process discovery.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

We fix a finite alphabet of activity labels $\Sigma$. Throughout the paper, activity labels are \emph{canonicalized} to $\mathrm{A0}, \mathrm{A1}, \ldots$ in order of first occurrence, making all representations invariant to arbitrary activity naming while preserving repeated-activity identity.

\paragraph{Event logs.}
A \emph{trace} is a finite word $\sigma = \langle a_1, \ldots, a_m \rangle$ with $a_j \in \Sigma$; an \emph{event log} is a finite multiset of traces, $L = [\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n] \in \mathcal{B}(\Sigma^*)$, preserving trace frequencies. Event logs carry only control-flow information here, abstracting from richer event attributes.

\paragraph{Process trees.}
A \emph{process tree}~\cite{Leemans2013} is a block-structured process model generated by the grammar
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
T \;::=\;& a \;\mid\; \tau \;\mid\; \operatorname{SEQ}(T_1,\ldots,T_k) \;\mid\; \operatorname{XOR}(T_1,\ldots,T_k)\\
&\mid\; \operatorname{AND}(T_1,\ldots,T_k) \;\mid\; \operatorname{LOOP}(T_{\mathit{body}},T_{\mathit{redo}})
\end{split}
\label{eq:grammar}
\end{equation}
with $k \ge 2$, where $a \in \Sigma$ is a visible activity, $\tau$ is a silent step, $\operatorname{SEQ}$ executes its children in order, $\operatorname{XOR}$ selects exactly one child, $\operatorname{AND}$ interleaves its children, and $\operatorname{LOOP}$ repeats a body through a redo branch. Every process tree can be converted into a sound workflow Petri net~\cite{Leemans2013}. Since $\operatorname{XOR}$ and $\operatorname{AND}$ are commutative, their children are sorted by a canonical structural key, giving behaviorally identical permutations one canonical representation.

\paragraph{Petri nets.}
An \emph{accepting labeled Petri net}~\cite{vanDerAalst2016} is a tuple $\mathit{PN} = (P, T, F, \ell, M_0, M_f)$ with places $P$, transitions $T$ ($P \cap T = \emptyset$), flow relation $F \subseteq (P \times T) \cup (T \times P)$, labeling $\ell : T \to \Sigma \cup \{\tau\}$, and initial and final markings $M_0, M_f$. For neural encoding, a Petri net is represented as a \emph{typed graph}: each node carries a type (place, visible transition, or invisible transition) and its token counts in $M_0$ and $M_f$; each arc carries a direction type. Nodes are sorted by name before tensorization, making the representation deterministic.

\section{The ProcRosetta Framework}
\label{sec:approach}

This section presents the paired training data, the encoders, the grammar-masked decoder, and the training objective, briefly explaining the neural building blocks where they appear.

\subsection{Synthetic Paired Triples}
\label{sec:datagen}

ProcRosetta is trained on synthetic \emph{paired triples} whose correspondence is fixed by the generator (Figure~\ref{fig:triple}): each sample $x_i = (T_i, L_i, G_i, e_i)$ belongs to one behavior family $e_i$ with a canonical process tree $T_i$.\footnote{Data-generation supplement: \url{https://github.com/fit-alessandro-berti/proc-rosetta/blob/main/data_generation.pdf}.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item canonical ordinary process trees are sampled from $\{\operatorname{SEQ}, \operatorname{XOR}, \operatorname{AND}, \operatorname{LOOP}\}$ process trees (maximum recursion depth $8$, arity $2$ to $3$ for non-loop operators, two-child loops, at most $30$ activities, reuse probability $0.15$) and canonicalized (Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries});
  \item each behavior is stored in two rows with the same process-tree target and the same visible traces, but with different Petri net graphs; the second graph may simply rename nodes or use different internal routing while preserving the same behavior;
  \item $L_i$ is a $128$-trace event-log view sampled from the family's visible behavior, and $G_i$ is the typed-graph representation of the corresponding Petri net variant.
\end{itemize}
The generator uses a fixed random seed and produces three disjoint family-wise splits: $8{,}192$ training, $1{,}024$ validation, and $1{,}024$ test rows, corresponding to $4{,}096$, $512$, and $512$ behavior families (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). Loops are enabled in the ordinary process-tree family. Since loop languages are generally unbounded, ordinary loop families use process tree playout to construct the finite observed trace pool, while their alternate Petri net representation is certified by isomorphic renaming. The controlled duplicate-label, concurrency, and non-free-choice families remain checked by bounded complete visible-language enumeration. The aggregate statistics and empirical results below use these regenerated splits and the corresponding epoch-36 checkpoint.\footnote{The data used to train and test the model, together with the latest checkpoint, are available at \url{https://github.com/fit-alessandro-berti/proc-rosetta/tree/main/dist_data}.}

Each behavior family contributes two rows with identical process tree and event-log views; only the Petri net graph may differ. Thus, the $1{,}024$-row test split contains $512$ family identifiers, and every row has a behaviorally equivalent companion in row-level neighborhood and retrieval analyses. Family-wise splitting prevents the two rows of one generated family from crossing splits, but it does not enforce visible-language uniqueness across independently generated families. Ordinary loop families use isomorphic node renaming for the alternate Petri view, whereas non-isomorphic routing variations occur in the controlled families; within-family Petri agreement therefore reflects identifier invariance throughout and invariance to alternative routing only for a subset.

For neural processing, each artifact is \emph{tokenized}. A process tree becomes a prefix sequence over a $40$-token vocabulary (special tokens, operators, arities, and activities $\mathrm{A0}, \ldots, \mathrm{A29}$), where each operator is followed by an explicit arity token and then its children (Figure~\ref{fig:triple}b). Traces are sequences over the $31$-token activity vocabulary. The Petri net graph is tensorized into node-type indices, marking features, and two directed adjacency matrices (one per arc direction).

\subsection{Three Encoders, One Latent Space}
\label{sec:encoders}

Each encoder maps its artifact to a diagonal Gaussian $q_m(z \mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_m, \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_m^2))$ over a shared $D_z$-dimensional latent space ($D_z = 256$), for modality $m \in \{\mathit{tree}, \mathit{trace}, \mathit{petri}\}$, as in variational autoencoders~\cite{KingmaWelling2014}: $\mu_m$ is the embedding of the artifact and $\sigma_m$ the encoder's uncertainty. During training, latent vectors are sampled as $z_m = \mu_m + \epsilon \odot \sigma_m$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, which regularizes the space; at test time the deterministic mean $z_m = \mu_m$ is used.

\emph{Process-tree encoder.} The token sequence of the process tree is embedded and processed by a gated recurrent unit (GRU), a standard recurrent network that reads a sequence left to right while maintaining a hidden state, with hidden size $H = 96$; the hidden state at the last non-padding token is projected linearly to $(\mu_{\mathit{tree}}, \sigma_{\mathit{tree}})$.

\emph{Event-log encoder.} The event-log encoder is \emph{hierarchical}, mirroring the event-log/trace/event structure: a GRU first encodes each trace into a trace vector; an attention mechanism then computes a data-dependent weight for every trace, emphasizing informative trace variants rather than averaging uniformly, and pools the trace vectors into a single event-log vector, projected to $(\mu_{\mathit{trace}}, \sigma_{\mathit{trace}})$.

\emph{Petri net encoder.} The Petri net encoder is a message-passing graph network. Each node starts from an embedding of its type (place / visible transition / invisible transition), its visible transition label when present, and a linear encoding of its marking feature; in each of three rounds, every node updates from its own state and its incoming and outgoing neighbors, followed by a nonlinearity and layer normalization. Mean-pooling yields the graph vector, projected to $(\mu_{\mathit{petri}}, \sigma_{\mathit{petri}})$.

When several views are available, ProcRosetta also defines a \emph{fused} embedding, the mean $\mu_{\mathit{fused}} = \tfrac{1}{3}(\mu_{\mathit{tree}} + \mu_{\mathit{trace}} + \mu_{\mathit{petri}})$.

\subsection{Grammar-Masked Process Tree Decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}

A single decoder, shared by all three modalities, is an autoregressive GRU: the latent vector $z$ initializes its hidden state, and tokens are generated one at a time, each step predicting a distribution over the vocabulary given the tokens so far.

An unconstrained decoder could produce malformed sequences. ProcRosetta therefore masks tokens using the prefix grammar. The state tracks open node slots and pending arity: after $\texttt{<bos>}$ one node is open; an activity or $\tau$ closes a slot; an operator must be followed by $\mathrm{ARITY}\_k$, which opens $k$ child slots; when none remain, only $\texttt{<eos>}$ is valid. Grammar-invalid logits are set to $-10^9$, giving invalid continuations zero probability. Consequently, every terminated output parses into a process tree of grammar~(\ref{eq:grammar}) and converts deterministically to a sound Petri net~\cite{Geng2023}.

\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:training}

Training uses \emph{teacher forcing}: the decoder receives the ground-truth token sequence shifted by one position and learns to predict every next token. Since all three artifacts of a triple share the same target process tree, each mini-batch trains three decoding pathways through the same decoder: reconstruction ($z_{\mathit{tree}} \to T$) and two cross-modal translations ($z_{\mathit{trace}} \to T$, $z_{\mathit{petri}} \to T$), via grammar-masked sequence cross-entropies ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{tree}\to\mathit{tree}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{trace}\to\mathit{tree}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{petri}\to\mathit{tree}}$) with label smoothing $0.08$ distributed only over grammar-valid tokens.

Two objectives shape the latent space. The \emph{alignment loss} penalizes disagreement between the latent means of the same sample,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{align}} = \tfrac{1}{3}\big( \lVert\mu_{\mathit{tree}}-\mu_{\mathit{trace}}\rVert_2^2 + \lVert\mu_{\mathit{tree}}-\mu_{\mathit{petri}}\rVert_2^2 + \lVert\mu_{\mathit{trace}}-\mu_{\mathit{petri}}\rVert_2^2 \big),
\end{equation}
pulling the three views of one process toward the same point. The \emph{contrastive loss}, in the style of CLIP~\cite{Radford2021}, additionally pushes \emph{different} processes apart: for every modality pair within a mini-batch, a symmetric multi-positive InfoNCE loss over cosine similarities (temperature $0.2$) rewards every row with the same behavior-family identifier as a positive. Finally, a weak KL regularizer $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{KL}}$ toward a standard normal prior~\cite{KingmaWelling2014} keeps the latent space bounded and smooth. The total loss is the weighted sum
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{tree}\to\mathit{tree}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathit{trace}\to\mathit{tree}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathit{petri}\to\mathit{tree}}}_{\text{reconstruction and translation}} + \; 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{align}} + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{contrastive}} + 0.001\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{KL}}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:hyper} summarizes the configuration. The model is deliberately small ($\approx$0.44M parameters), with standard safeguards against overfitting: dropout, weight decay, label smoothing, stochastic latent sampling, family-grouped positives, activity remapping, validation-based learning-rate reduction, early stopping, and per-epoch checkpointing.

\section{Assessment}
\label{sec:assessment}

The assessment follows the three research questions, covering training behavior, decode quality (RQ2), process discovery quality against the Inductive Miner (RQ3), embedding quality against baselines (RQ1, RQ3), and cross-modal retrieval (RQ1). All results are computed on the held-out test split of $1{,}024$ triples ($512$ behavior families), used neither for optimization nor for model selection, with the final epoch-36 checkpoint and deterministic latent means.

\subsection{Training Behavior}
\label{sec:trainingbehavior}



Across 36 epochs (Figure~\ref{fig:training}), validation loss decreases from $3.521$ to its minimum, $2.920$, at epoch 35; the learning rate is halved entering epoch 30. Tables~\ref{tab:decode} through~\ref{tab:retrieval} nevertheless use the final epoch-36 checkpoint distributed as the default, distinguishing the best-validation epoch from the reported checkpoint; the test split played no role in selection. Validation loss is below training loss because only training uses dropout, stochastic latents, grouped positives, and activity remapping; validation and testing use deterministic means on unremapped inputs. Process-tree-reconstruction validation loss falls from $1.100$ to $0.924$ and contrastive loss from $1.917$ to $1.319$. The final-checkpoint test loss is $2.4893$ (process tree $0.7942$, trace$\to$process tree $0.8009$, Petri net$\to$process tree $0.7970$, contrastive $0.8092$, KL $15.4451$); as a descriptive result on another fixed split, it is not a model-selection comparison with validation loss. The run takes about 160 minutes on the configured CUDA device.

\subsection{Process Discovery Quality (RQ3)}
\label{sec:discoveryresults}



Table~\ref{tab:discovery} compares ProcRosetta event-log encoding/process-tree decoding with PM4Py's Inductive Miner on each test event log. PM4Py footprints are computed directly from the event log and discovered process tree, not from a converted Petri net, and yield fitness and precision.

The Inductive Miner attains perfect footprint fitness and F1 $0.958$. ProcRosetta attains fitness $0.866$, precision $0.897$, and F1 $0.866$ (about 90\% of the baseline), with 99.8\% model success and 96.5\% footprint evaluability. Its purely neural translation compresses an event log into one 256-dimensional vector without replay or search, but the gap shows that validity does not recover every observed ordering relation. ProcRosetta is therefore a learned translation and representation mechanism, not a replacement for conformance-optimized deterministic process discovery.

Table~\ref{tab:embedding} ranks the four learned ProcRosetta representations against six deterministic baselines and the learned PetriNet2Vec baseline~\cite{Colonna2024}. Three observations stand out. First, deterministic event-log abstractions are strongest globally on this structured synthetic test set: directly-follows, PM4Py case features, eventually-follows, and activity counts reach $\rho = 0.896$ to $0.927$. The directly-follows and trace-variant baselines still deserve a caveat: the behavioral distance is \emph{defined} as an average that includes their $L_1$ abstractions, so they partially measure the evaluation target by construction and should be read as skyline references rather than as competing methods.

Second, ProcRosetta's learned event-log embedding is the strongest ProcRosetta view at $\rho = 0.747$, below the deterministic event-log features but above the fused, Petri-net, and process-tree views and the high-dimensional trace-variant baseline. Its zero nearest-neighbor behavioral distance should be interpreted in light of the paired-row construction. For event-log- and process-tree-based representations, the companion row is encoded from an identical input; for Petri net representations, the result indicates whether the alternate Petri-net view is placed nearby. The metric therefore characterizes same-family locality rather than discrimination among $1{,}024$ distinct behaviors.

Third, the Petri net comparison exposes a global versus local trade-off. ProcRosetta more often places the paired same-family Petri variant nearest, whereas PetriNet2Vec more accurately preserves the global ordering of pairwise behavioral distances ($\rho = 0.817$ versus $0.671$; NN behavior $0.632$ versus $0.000$).

The components retain $51.3\%$ of variance, so Tables~\ref{tab:embedding} and~\ref{tab:agreement} remain the full-space evidence.

The modality-specific embeddings are strongly compatible with their arithmetic-mean fusion, with pairwise-distance correlations of $0.824$ to $0.970$. Because the fused representation is constructed directly from the same three modality embeddings, Table~\ref{tab:agreement} is a geometric consistency check rather than independent validation of alignment. The cross-modal retrieval results in Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} provide the more direct test of whether representations from different artifact types can identify their paired counterparts.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Retrieval (RQ1)}
\label{sec:retrievalresults}



Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} directly tests cross-modal alignment by retrieving the matching row among 1{,}024 candidates in all six directions. Top-1 accuracy is 2.9 to 8.0\% (chance: 0.1\%) and mean rank 96.7 to 100.8 (random expectation: 512). Exact-row scores are deliberately strict: each query has one indexed target, although its alternate row is behaviorally equivalent. Separately, the nearest row is from the same family in 25.0\% of fused, 25.0\% of Petri-net, 74.6\% of event-log, and 25.6\% of process-tree embeddings (within-family cosine: 0.995 to 1.000). Thus instance-level alignment is above chance but leaves substantial retrieval headroom.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Implementation}
\label{sec:repository}

The public Python implementation uses PyTorch and PM4Py~\cite{Berti2023PM4Py}: \url{https://github.com/fit-alessandro-berti/proc-rosetta}. Its generator, training and evaluation pipelines, and metric implementations reproduce the study end-to-end: \texttt{sample.py} regenerates Table~\ref{tab:dataset}'s seeded splits, \texttt{train.py} trains Table~\ref{tab:hyper}'s model and writes Figure~\ref{fig:training}'s metrics, and \texttt{test.py} produces Tables~\ref{tab:decode} through~\ref{tab:retrieval}. Utilities also embed external \texttt{.xes}, \texttt{.pnml}, and \texttt{.ptml} artifacts and decode event logs or Petri nets into process trees with automatic canonicalization and label restoration.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
Three threats qualify these findings. \emph{(i) Synthetic data.} Training and evaluation use synthetic behavior families that do not capture the noise and complexity of real event logs, limiting conclusions to in-distribution artifacts. Family-wise splitting keeps paired rows together but does not prevent visible-language overlap between independently generated families, which may inflate exact-match rates. \emph{(ii) Metric circularity.} The behavioral distance shares two of its three components with the directly-follows and trace-variant baselines, favoring their scores; Table~\ref{tab:embedding} therefore also reports the less affected nearest-neighbor quality. \emph{(iii) Single configuration.} Results cover one seeded, full-objective configuration; variation across seeds and hyperparameters and the contributions of individual objectives remain unmeasured.

\paragraph{Scope of the model class.}
The target process trees cover sequence, choice, concurrency, and two-child loops. Some Petri net inputs are non-block or non-free-choice variants, but the decoder still emits block-structured process trees, which are less expressive than partially ordered formalisms such as POWL~\cite{Kourani2026}. Any target language with a prefix grammar, canonical form, and deterministic Petri-net conversion could replace the decoder.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ProcRosetta provides a unified representation for event logs, process trees, and Petri nets, connecting complementary views of process behavior. The study shows that these artifacts can be brought into a shared latent space while retaining a grammar-constrained path back to process models. Cross-modal retrieval, reconstruction, discovery, and neighborhood analyses suggest that the representation captures meaningful behavioral relationships, although alignment across modalities and generalization beyond the training distribution remain open challenges.

Future work should broaden the data and model families, evaluate robustness across settings, incorporate richer sound target languages, and develop objectives and conformance-aware measures for transferable cross-modal process understanding.
