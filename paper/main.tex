\begin{abstract}
Process mining revolves around heterogeneous artifacts: event logs record observed behavior, while process trees and Petri nets specify intended or discovered behavior. Every established technique that connects two of these artifact types---process discovery, conformance checking, model transformation---is a dedicated algorithm for one direction of one artifact pair. This paper presents \emph{ProcRosetta}, a multimodal neural framework that embeds event logs, process trees, and Petri nets into a single shared latent space of process behavior and decodes any point of that space into a syntactically valid process tree. Three artifact-specific encoders (a recurrent tree encoder, a hierarchical attention-based log encoder, and a message-passing Petri-net encoder) are trained jointly with a shared grammar-masked decoder on synthetic paired triples, using reconstruction, cross-modal translation, latent alignment, contrastive, and variational objectives; grammar masking and deterministic tree-to-net conversion make every completed decode well-formed by construction. On a held-out test split of 1{,}024 process triples grouped into 512 behavior families, at least 99.8\% of decodes from every latent source terminate as valid, Petri-convertible trees, event-log translation reaches a footprint-conformance F1 of 0.866 (compared with 0.958 for the Inductive Miner), and all six cross-modal retrieval directions perform above chance. The strongest learned log embedding reaches a behavioral-distance rank correlation of 0.747 and retrieves an exact-equivalent nearest neighbor on average, although deterministic log features remain stronger globally. The implementation, data generator, and evaluation suite are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Process mining connects observed event data with formal models~\cite{vanDerAalst2016} and moves between different \emph{representations} of the same behavior: logs sample executed traces, process trees specify block-structured behavior, and Petri nets make states and concurrency explicit. Existing bridges are direction-specific algorithms: discovery maps logs to models~\cite{Aalst2004,Leemans2013}, conformance checking relates logs and models, and transformations connect model notations. What is missing is a common \emph{computational representation} in which equivalent artifacts are nearby regardless of type. Such a space would support cross-artifact search, clustering, machine learning, and translation by decoding. Shared embeddings are effective in multimodal learning~\cite{Radford2021}; process mining has learned representations for traces and logs~\cite{DeKoninck2018,Barbon2023} and Petri nets~\cite{Colonna2024}, but these remain single-modality and do not generate models from the learned space.

ProcRosetta treats logs, process trees, and Petri nets as three views of one behavior. Artifact-specific encoders map them into a shared latent space, alignment and contrastive objectives align matching views, and one decoder maps any latent vector to a process tree. The decoder is \emph{grammar-masked}: only tokens that keep the partial output syntactically valid are allowed~\cite{Geng2023}. Every completed tree converts deterministically to a Petri net with PM4Py~\cite{Berti2023PM4Py}, making successfully terminated outputs well-formed and sound by construction. The name evokes the Rosetta stone: training presents the same content in three ``languages'' and learns their correspondence.

Figure~\ref{fig:outline} summarizes the framework. Three research questions organize the investigation:

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] Can event logs, process trees, and Petri nets be embedded into a shared latent space in which the three views of the same process behavior receive nearby representations?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] Can a learned model translate event logs or Petri nets into \emph{valid} process trees---well-formed, Petri-convertible models whose behavior is close to the source?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] How do the learned representations and the learned log-to-model translation compare with classical baselines: deterministic log and model features, Petri-net embeddings, and the Inductive Miner?
\end{itemize}

The contributions are: (i)~a multimodal architecture with three artifact-specific encoders projecting into one latent space of process behavior; (ii)~a shared, grammar-masked process-tree decoder that guarantees syntactic validity of completed outputs and, through deterministic conversion, yields sound Petri nets; (iii)~a training procedure over synthetic paired triples combining reconstruction, cross-modal translation, latent alignment, contrastive, and variational objectives; and (iv)~a comprehensive assessment with a publicly available implementation, covering decode quality, discovery quality against the Inductive Miner, behavior-aligned embedding quality, and cross-modal retrieval.

Section~\ref{sec:relatedWork} reviews related work; Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} introduces the process-mining objects; Section~\ref{sec:approach} presents the framework; Section~\ref{sec:tool} presents the interactive tool support; Section~\ref{sec:assessment} reports the assessment; Sections~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss and conclude.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedWork}

\paragraph{Discovery and model generation.}
Classical discovery maps logs to models~\cite{Aalst2004}. The Inductive Miner recursively decomposes logs into sound, block-structured process trees~\cite{Leemans2013}; more expressive sound targets such as POWL relax strict block structure~\cite{Kourani2026}. Neural discovery has learned a direct log-to-Petri-net mapping from synthetic pairs~\cite{Sommers2021}. ProcRosetta retains the process-tree bias for validity and deterministic Petri conversion, but learns a tri-modal space rather than one log-to-model direction.

\paragraph{Representation learning.}
Prior process embeddings cover traces and logs~\cite{DeKoninck2018,Barbon2023} or Petri nets~\cite{Colonna2024}; PetriNet2Vec is therefore a baseline below. They encode one artifact family and do not decode models from a shared space. ProcRosetta instead aligns three artifact types and evaluates both latent geometry and generated-model behavior.

Model comparison distinguishes label, structural, and behavioral similarity~\cite{Dijkman2011}, while alignment-based conformance provides exact log--model diagnostics~\cite{Adriansyah2012}. ProcRosetta's latent distance is a scalable but approximate proxy, so the assessment separately measures behavioral-distance agreement and discovered-model conformance.

\paragraph{Shared representations and constrained decoding.}
CLIP established contrastive alignment of paired modalities~\cite{Radford2021}, while grammar-constrained decoding enforces structured outputs~\cite{Geng2023}. ProcRosetta combines these ideas for formal artifacts: equivalence is behavioral, and the shared decoder emits grammar-valid process trees. Tri-modal alignment, behavioral evaluation, and validity-preserving generation distinguish it from both single-modality embeddings and direct neural discovery.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

We fix a finite alphabet of activity labels $\Sigma$. Throughout the paper, activity labels are \emph{canonicalized} to $\mathrm{A0}, \mathrm{A1}, \ldots$ in order of first occurrence, making all representations invariant to arbitrary activity naming while preserving repeated-activity identity.

\paragraph{Event logs.}
A \emph{trace} is a finite word $\sigma = \langle a_1, \ldots, a_m \rangle$ with $a_j \in \Sigma$; an \emph{event log} is a finite multiset of traces, $L = [\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n] \in \mathcal{B}(\Sigma^*)$, preserving trace frequencies. Logs carry only control-flow information here, abstracting from richer event attributes.

\paragraph{Process trees.}
A \emph{process tree}~\cite{Leemans2013} is a block-structured process model generated by the grammar
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
T \;::=\;& a \;\mid\; \tau \;\mid\; \operatorname{SEQ}(T_1,\ldots,T_k) \;\mid\; \operatorname{XOR}(T_1,\ldots,T_k)\\
&\mid\; \operatorname{AND}(T_1,\ldots,T_k) \;\mid\; \operatorname{LOOP}(T_{\mathit{body}},T_{\mathit{redo}})
\end{split}
\label{eq:grammar}
\end{equation}
with $k \ge 2$, where $a \in \Sigma$ is a visible activity, $\tau$ is a silent step, $\operatorname{SEQ}$ executes its children in order, $\operatorname{XOR}$ selects exactly one child, $\operatorname{AND}$ interleaves its children, and $\operatorname{LOOP}$ repeats a body through a redo branch. Every process tree can be converted into a sound workflow net~\cite{Leemans2013}. Since $\operatorname{XOR}$ and $\operatorname{AND}$ are commutative, their children are sorted by a canonical structural key, giving behaviorally identical permutations one canonical representation.

\paragraph{Petri nets.}
An \emph{accepting labeled Petri net}~\cite{vanDerAalst2016} is a tuple $\mathit{PN} = (P, T, F, \ell, M_0, M_f)$ with places $P$, transitions $T$ ($P \cap T = \emptyset$), flow relation $F \subseteq (P \times T) \cup (T \times P)$, labeling $\ell : T \to \Sigma \cup \{\tau\}$, and initial and final markings $M_0, M_f$. For neural encoding, a Petri net is represented as a \emph{typed graph}: each node carries a type (place, visible transition, or invisible transition) and its token counts in $M_0$ and $M_f$; each arc carries a direction type. Nodes are sorted by name before tensorization, making the representation deterministic.

\section{The ProcRosetta Framework}
\label{sec:approach}

This section presents the paired training data, the encoders, the grammar-masked decoder, and the training objective, briefly explaining the neural building blocks where they appear.

\subsection{Synthetic Paired Triples}
\label{sec:datagen}

ProcRosetta is trained on synthetic \emph{paired triples} whose correspondence is fixed by the generator (Figure~\ref{fig:triple}): each sample $x_i = (T_i, L_i, G_i, e_i)$ belongs to one behavior family $e_i$ with a canonical process tree $T_i$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item canonical ordinary trees are sampled from $\{\operatorname{SEQ}, \operatorname{XOR}, \operatorname{AND}, \operatorname{LOOP}\}$ trees (maximum recursion depth $8$, arity $2$--$3$ for non-loop operators, two-child loops, at most $30$ activities, reuse probability $0.15$) and canonicalized (Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries});
  \item each behavior is stored in two rows with the same tree target and the same visible traces, but with different Petri-net graphs; the second graph may simply rename nodes or use different internal routing while preserving the same behavior;
  \item $L_i$ is a $128$-trace log view sampled from the family's visible behavior, and $G_i$ is the typed-graph representation of the corresponding Petri-net variant.
\end{itemize}
The generator uses a fixed random seed and produces three disjoint family-wise splits: $8{,}192$ training, $1{,}024$ validation, and $1{,}024$ test rows, corresponding to $4{,}096$, $512$, and $512$ behavior families (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). Loops are enabled in the ordinary-tree family. Since loop languages are generally unbounded, ordinary loop families use process-tree playout to construct the finite observed trace pool, while their alternate Petri-net representation is certified by isomorphic renaming. The controlled duplicate-label, concurrency, and non-free-choice families remain checked by bounded complete visible-language enumeration. The aggregate statistics and empirical results below use these regenerated splits and the corresponding epoch-36 checkpoint.

For neural processing, each artifact is \emph{tokenized}. A process tree becomes a prefix sequence over a $40$-token vocabulary (special tokens, operators, arities, and activities $\mathrm{A0}, \ldots, \mathrm{A29}$), where each operator is followed by an explicit arity token and then its children (Figure~\ref{fig:triple}b). Traces are sequences over the $31$-token activity vocabulary. The Petri net graph is tensorized into node-type indices, marking features, and two directed adjacency matrices (one per arc direction).

\subsection{Three Encoders, One Latent Space}
\label{sec:encoders}

Each encoder maps its artifact to a diagonal Gaussian $q_m(z \mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_m, \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_m^2))$ over a shared $D_z$-dimensional latent space ($D_z = 256$), for modality $m \in \{\mathit{tree}, \mathit{trace}, \mathit{petri}\}$, as in variational autoencoders~\cite{KingmaWelling2014}: $\mu_m$ is the embedding of the artifact and $\sigma_m$ the encoder's uncertainty. During training, latent vectors are sampled as $z_m = \mu_m + \epsilon \odot \sigma_m$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, which regularizes the space; at test time the deterministic mean $z_m = \mu_m$ is used.

\emph{Tree encoder.} The token sequence of the process tree is embedded and processed by a gated recurrent unit (GRU)---a standard recurrent network that reads a sequence left to right while maintaining a hidden state---with hidden size $H = 96$; the hidden state at the last non-padding token is projected linearly to $(\mu_{\mathit{tree}}, \sigma_{\mathit{tree}})$.

\emph{Log encoder.} The event-log encoder is \emph{hierarchical}, mirroring the log/trace/event structure: a GRU first encodes each trace into a trace vector; an attention mechanism then computes a data-dependent weight for every trace---emphasizing informative trace variants rather than averaging uniformly---and pools the trace vectors into a single log vector, projected to $(\mu_{\mathit{trace}}, \sigma_{\mathit{trace}})$.

\emph{Petri-net encoder.} The Petri-net encoder is a message-passing graph network. Each node starts from an embedding of its type (place / visible transition / invisible transition), its visible transition label when present, and a linear encoding of its marking feature; in each of three rounds, every node updates from its own state and its incoming and outgoing neighbors, followed by a nonlinearity and layer normalization. Mean-pooling yields the graph vector, projected to $(\mu_{\mathit{petri}}, \sigma_{\mathit{petri}})$.

When several views are available, ProcRosetta also defines a \emph{fused} embedding, the mean $\mu_{\mathit{fused}} = \tfrac{1}{3}(\mu_{\mathit{tree}} + \mu_{\mathit{trace}} + \mu_{\mathit{petri}})$.

\subsection{Grammar-Masked Process-Tree Decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}

A single decoder, shared by all three modalities, is an autoregressive GRU: the latent vector $z$ initializes its hidden state, and tokens are generated one at a time, each step predicting a distribution over the vocabulary given the tokens so far.

An unconstrained decoder could produce malformed sequences. ProcRosetta therefore masks tokens using the prefix grammar. The state tracks open node slots and pending arity: after $\texttt{<bos>}$ one node is open; an activity or $\tau$ closes a slot; an operator must be followed by $\mathrm{ARITY}\_k$, which opens $k$ child slots; when none remain, only $\texttt{<eos>}$ is valid. Grammar-invalid logits are set to $-10^9$, giving invalid continuations zero probability. Consequently, every terminated output parses into a tree of grammar~(\ref{eq:grammar}) and converts deterministically to a sound Petri net~\cite{Geng2023}.

\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:training}

Training uses \emph{teacher forcing}: the decoder receives the ground-truth token sequence shifted by one position and learns to predict every next token. Since all three artifacts of a triple share the same target tree, each mini-batch trains three decoding pathways through the same decoder---reconstruction ($z_{\mathit{tree}} \to T$) and two cross-modal translations ($z_{\mathit{trace}} \to T$, $z_{\mathit{petri}} \to T$)---via grammar-masked sequence cross-entropies ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{tree}\to\mathit{tree}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{trace}\to\mathit{tree}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{petri}\to\mathit{tree}}$) with label smoothing $0.08$ distributed only over grammar-valid tokens.

Two objectives shape the latent space. The \emph{alignment loss} penalizes disagreement between the latent means of the same sample,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{align}} = \tfrac{1}{3}\big( \lVert\mu_{\mathit{tree}}-\mu_{\mathit{trace}}\rVert_2^2 + \lVert\mu_{\mathit{tree}}-\mu_{\mathit{petri}}\rVert_2^2 + \lVert\mu_{\mathit{trace}}-\mu_{\mathit{petri}}\rVert_2^2 \big),
\end{equation}
pulling the three views of one process toward the same point. The \emph{contrastive loss}, in the style of CLIP~\cite{Radford2021}, additionally pushes \emph{different} processes apart: for every modality pair within a mini-batch, a symmetric multi-positive InfoNCE loss over cosine similarities (temperature $0.2$) rewards every row with the same behavior-family identifier as a positive. Finally, a weak KL regularizer $\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{KL}}$ toward a standard normal prior~\cite{KingmaWelling2014} keeps the latent space bounded and smooth. The total loss is the weighted sum
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{tree}\to\mathit{tree}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathit{trace}\to\mathit{tree}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathit{petri}\to\mathit{tree}}}_{\text{reconstruction and translation}} + \; 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{align}} + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{contrastive}} + 0.001\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathit{KL}}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:hyper} summarizes the configuration. The model is deliberately small ($\approx$0.44M parameters), with standard safeguards against overfitting: dropout, weight decay, label smoothing, stochastic latent sampling, family-grouped positives, activity remapping, validation-based learning-rate reduction, early stopping, and best-validation checkpoint retention.

\section{Interactive Tool Support}
\label{sec:tool}

ProcRosetta is accompanied by an interactive Streamlit tool that turns the framework into an inspectable workflow. Users can collect event logs, process trees, and Petri nets in a common workspace, encode individual or grouped artifacts, and translate their representations back into process trees. The translation view presents the generated tree, its validity checks, the deterministically derived Petri net, and a comparison with the source artifact. A complementary latent-space view supports projections, similarity and group-agreement inspection, nearest-neighbor exploration, and interpolation. The tool also provides checkpoint summaries, guided evaluation, and export of embeddings and generated models. Figure~\ref{fig:tool} illustrates the translation and latent-space views.

\section{Assessment}
\label{sec:assessment}

The assessment follows the three research questions, covering training behavior, decode quality (RQ2), discovery quality against the Inductive Miner (RQ3), embedding quality against baselines (RQ1, RQ3), and cross-modal retrieval (RQ1). All results are computed on the held-out test split of $1{,}024$ triples ($512$ behavior families)---used neither for optimization nor for model selection---with the final epoch-36 checkpoint and deterministic latent means.

\subsection{Training Behavior}
\label{sec:trainingbehavior}



Figure~\ref{fig:training} shows the current training run. Across 36 completed epochs, the validation loss decreases from $3.521$ to a best value of $2.920$ at epoch 35; the schedule halves the learning rate once, entering epoch 30, and the final epoch-36 checkpoint is used for the test report below. The validation loss is lower than the training loss because training uses dropout, stochastic latent sampling, grouped positives, and activity remapping, whereas validation and testing use deterministic latent means and unremapped inputs. The principal validation components improve together (Figure~\ref{fig:training}b): tree reconstruction falls from $1.100$ to $0.924$, while the contrastive loss falls from $1.917$ to $1.319$. \subsection{Process-Discovery Quality (RQ3)}
\label{sec:discoveryresults}



Table~\ref{tab:discovery} addresses the log-to-model use case: each test log is translated by ProcRosetta (encode the log and decode a tree) and, independently, mined with PM4Py's Inductive Miner. The current report computes PM4Py footprints directly from each log and process tree, rather than from converted Petri nets, and reports footprint fitness and precision.

The Inductive Miner achieves perfect footprint fitness and an F1 of 0.958. ProcRosetta reaches fitness 0.866, precision 0.897, and F1 0.866---about 90\% of the Inductive Miner's F1. Its model-success rate is 99.8\%, and footprints are evaluable for 96.5\% of test logs. The purely neural translation compresses the entire log into a single 256-dimensional vector, without replay or search, but the gap shows that validity alone does not recover all observed ordering relations. We therefore treat ProcRosetta as a learned translation and representation mechanism, not a replacement for a deterministic discovery algorithm optimized for conformance.

Figure~\ref{fig:discoveryexamples} provides an out-of-distribution comparison on two supplied event logs. The ProcRosetta trees are reconstructed directly from the two tool screenshots; no activity label is abbreviated (long labels are only line-wrapped). Separately from the footprint-based test-set table, these external examples retain alignment-based scoring on the same frequency-preserving input sample: 128 traces for \texttt{receipt.xes} and all 100 traces for \texttt{roadtraffic100traces.xes}. On the receipt log, the 16-node screenshot tree retains 10 activities and reaches alignment F1 0.193, compared with 0.483 for the Inductive Miner's 47-node tree. On road traffic, the 16-node screenshot tree contains all ten activities but reaches F1 0.202, whereas the Inductive Miner reaches 0.850. The two ProcRosetta outputs share a compact top-level-choice and nested-loop structure, but the low precision (0.125 in both cases) shows that arranging the labels into a valid tree is not enough to capture their ordering constraints. The deterministic miner fits the observed variants much more closely, so these examples support the aggregate in-distribution result without suggesting equivalent performance on external logs.

\subsection{Embedding Quality (RQ1, RQ3)}
\label{sec:embeddingresults}

If the latent space is a space of process \emph{behavior}, then distances in it should correlate with behavioral distances. The assessment quantifies the behavioral distance between two logs as the mean of three $L_1$ distances between empirical distributions extracted from the logs---trace variants, directly-follows relations (with boundary symbols)~\cite{vanDerAalst2019DFG}, and trace lengths---each in $[0,2]$. Across all $523{,}776$ pairs of test logs, this distance averages $1.2128$ (min $0.0000$, max $1.9980$). Each embedding method is scored by: (i)~the Spearman rank correlation $\rho$ between embedding cosine distance and behavioral distance over all pairs; (ii)~the mean behavioral distance from each sample to its \emph{nearest neighbor} in the embedding (NN behavior; lower is better); and (iii)~the improvement of the latter over the random-pair average (higher is better).

\subsection{Cross-Modal Retrieval (RQ1)}
\label{sec:retrievalresults}



The most direct test of cross-modal alignment is retrieval: given, e.g., a Petri net, does its embedding find the matching event log row among all 1{,}024 test rows? Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} reports top-1 accuracy, mean reciprocal rank (MRR), and the mean rank of the true counterpart, for all six directions. All directions perform above chance: top-1 accuracies are 2.9--8.0\% against a 0.1\% chance level, and mean ranks are 96.7--100.8 against an expected 512 for random ranking. These exact-row scores are deliberately strict in the behavior-family setting: each query has one indexed target row, but equivalent alternate rows may be behaviorally just as correct. The separate family-equivalence check reports within-family cosine similarity of 0.995--1.000 and family top-1 values of 0.250 (fused), 0.250 (Petri), 0.746 (trace), and 0.256 (tree). The results establish above-chance instance-level alignment while leaving clear headroom for exact cross-modal retrieval.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Implementation}
\label{sec:repository}

ProcRosetta is implemented in Python on top of PyTorch and PM4Py~\cite{Berti2023PM4Py}; the complete source code (synthetic data generator, training and evaluation pipelines, and all metric implementations) is publicly available at \url{https://github.com/fit-alessandro-berti/proc-rosetta}. Three commands reproduce the study end-to-end: \texttt{sample.py} regenerates the seeded splits of Table~\ref{tab:dataset}, \texttt{train.py} trains the model of Table~\ref{tab:hyper} and writes the per-epoch metrics of Figure~\ref{fig:training}, and \texttt{test.py} produces every number in Tables~\ref{tab:decode}--\ref{tab:retrieval}. The repository also provides utilities that apply a trained checkpoint to external artifacts (\texttt{.xes} event logs, \texttt{.pnml} Petri nets, \texttt{.ptml} process trees), computing embeddings and decoding logs or nets into process trees with automatic canonicalization and label restoration.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
Four threats qualify these findings. \emph{(i) Synthetic data.} Training and evaluation use synthetic behavior families with controlled alternate Petri-net representations and synthetic trace edits; this noise does not reproduce the incompleteness, deviating behavior, attributes, and long-tail variants of real logs, so the conclusions concern in-distribution artifacts (out-of-distribution quality is not claimed here). \emph{(ii) Metric circularity.} The behavioral distance used for embedding evaluation shares two of its three components with the directly-follows and trace-variant baselines, inflating their scores; Table~\ref{tab:embedding} marks them, and the less affected nearest-neighbor quality is reported alongside correlation. \emph{(iii) Duplicate behavior across splits.} Splits are family-wise, but the generator does not explicitly deduplicate visible languages across splits, so a fraction of test behaviors may resemble training behaviors; the exact-match rates of Table~\ref{tab:decode} should be read with this in mind. \emph{(iv) Single configuration.} All results come from one seeded data configuration and one trained model; variance across seeds and hyperparameter sensitivity are not quantified.

\paragraph{Scope of the model class.}
The target trees cover sequence, choice, concurrency, and two-child loops. Some Petri inputs are non-block or non-free-choice variants, but the decoder still emits block-structured process trees, which are less expressive than partially ordered formalisms such as POWL~\cite{Kourani2026}. Any target language with a prefix grammar, canonical form, and deterministic Petri conversion could replace the decoder.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced ProcRosetta, a multimodal neural framework that embeds the three central artifact types of process mining---event logs, process trees, and Petri nets---into a single latent space of process behavior and decodes points of that space into grammar-valid process trees that convert deterministically into sound Petri nets. The assessment answers the three research questions with qualifications: modality-specific geometries correlate $0.824$--$0.970$ with the fused geometry, and all exact-row retrieval directions exceed chance (RQ1); at least 99.8\% of decodes from every source terminate as valid, Petri-convertible trees, with 75.0\% exact match and decoded behavior substantially closer to the source than unrelated rows (RQ2); and ProcRosetta's log embedding preserves exact-equivalent nearest neighbors while deterministic features remain stronger in global rank correlation, whereas learned log-to-tree translation reaches about 90\% of the Inductive Miner's footprint F1 (RQ3).

Future work should scale data and encoders, add realistic incomplete and noisy logs, quantify cross-split near-duplicates, validate on real artifacts, strengthen retrieval and decoding, and measure multi-seed robustness. Richer sound targets such as POWL~\cite{Kourani2026}, exact-conformance distances, and further modalities such as natural-language descriptions would move closer to a true Rosetta stone for process representations.
